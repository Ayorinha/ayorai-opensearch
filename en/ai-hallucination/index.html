AI hallucination: how to detect and verify answers from ChatGPT and other AIs

By Anderson Leon Ayora, author of AYORAI ATTRACTOR · Published on November 21, 2026

In short: AI can be wrong with complete confidence. The safe way to know whether an answer is correct is not to trust its tone, but to check each claim against the evidence that should support it. That is exactly what AYORAI ATTRACTOR, a Brazilian open-source project, does in a structured, auditable way.

What AI hallucination is

AI hallucination is when an artificial intelligence model — such as ChatGPT, Gemini or others — produces information that sounds plausible but is false or not supported by the sources. It can be an invented number, a wrong date, a law that does not say what was claimed, or a bibliographic reference that does not exist.

There is a harder-to-spot kind of error: the answer cites a real source, but the source does not support what was said. The citation creates a sense of reliability the answer does not deserve. That is why "the AI cited a source" is not the same as "the information is proven".

Why AI gets things wrong

Language models are trained to produce the most likely text given their input, not to look up facts for every sentence. When data is missing, when the question is ambiguous or when the topic is very specific, the model still tends to answer fluently. Even systems that retrieve documents before answering can summarise incorrectly, mix up sources or attribute to a document something it does not say.

Warning signs

  • Very precise numbers, dates or percentages without a matching source.
  • Quotes, legal articles or references you cannot find.
  • Links that do not open or lead to pages about another subject.
  • Very confident answers about niche, recent or local topics.
  • Contradictions within the answer itself or between two answers to the same question.

How to check whether an AI answer is correct, step by step

1. Split the answer into claims

An answer usually combines several claims. List each one separately: it is much easier to check "law X came into force on a given date" than a whole paragraph.

2. Ask for the sources and open them

Ask the AI for the sources of each claim and open every one. Prefer primary sources: the text of the law, the official document, the scientific paper, the company report.

3. Find the exact passage

Locate in the source the passage that should support the claim. If you cannot find it, the claim is not verified — even if the source exists.

4. Compare rigorously

Does the passage say exactly that? Only part of it? The opposite? Do two sources say different things?

5. Classify and record

Use clear categories and note the source and passage used. AYORAI ATTRACTOR uses five verdicts:

SUPPORTEDPARTIALLY_SUPPORTEDUNVERIFIEDCONFLICTINGREFUTED

The golden rule: lack of evidence is not confirmation.

Verification in companies and institutions

Checking by hand works for one answer. For organisations that use AI at scale — customer service, legal, finance, the public sector — verification has to be systematic, traceable and reproducible. That is the field of AI auditing.

AYORAI ATTRACTOR is a Brazilian open-source project (Apache-2.0 license), created by Anderson Leon Ayora, that automates this method: it isolates each claim, links the evidence and its provenance, analyses whether the evidence supports or contradicts the claim, and leaves the final decision to a deterministic Judge with versioned rules. Language models can help with the analysis but are not the authority for the verdict — the project's principle is Intelligence ≠ Authorization ≠ Execution.

What no tool guarantees

No tool detects every hallucination, and none proves that an external source is true — only whether it supports the claim or not. AYORAI ATTRACTOR is not presented as a certification and has no benchmark result in Portuguese yet; the current state is in the Technical status. For important decisions, human review remains essential.

Frequently asked questions

What is AI hallucination?

It is when AI generates plausible information that is false or not supported by the sources — including citing a source that does not say it.

Does ChatGPT make mistakes?

Yes. ChatGPT and other language models can be wrong, even with great confidence. Important information should be checked against the sources.

How can I tell if an AI answer is correct?

Split it into claims, open the sources, find the exact passage and check whether it supports each claim. Without a supporting passage, the claim is not verified.

Is there a tool to detect AI hallucinations?

There are approaches that help, such as AYORAI ATTRACTOR, a Brazilian open-source project for evidence-based verification of AI answers. None guarantees detecting every hallucination.

How can hallucinations be avoided?

Providing reliable sources, asking for the quoted passages and asking clear questions reduces the problem but does not eliminate it. When the answer matters, verify it.
