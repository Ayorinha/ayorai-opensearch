Claim verification for AI answers: how it works and open-source tools

By Anderson Leon Ayora, author of AYORAI ATTRACTOR · Published on November 21, 2026

In short: claim verification means checking, one by one, whether the claims in an AI answer are supported by the sources used. It is the most direct way to find hallucinations. There are open-source models that do the check, such as MiniCheck and FactCG, and auditing layers, such as AYORAI ATTRACTOR, that record how each verdict was reached.

What claim verification is

An AI answer is rarely a single sentence. It combines several claims: a number, a date, a name, a rule. Claim verification separates these parts and asks, for each one: does the source support this, contradict this, or not allow a conclusion?

This is different from asking whether the answer "looks right" or whether it "cited a source". An answer can cite a real document and still claim something the document does not say.

Why verify claim by claim

  • The error is pinpointed. Instead of "the answer has a problem", you know which sentence lacks support.
  • A good source does not "vouch" for the whole paragraph. A correct passage should not automatically lend credibility to the sentences around it.
  • It can be audited later. Each verdict stays linked to the evidence passage that justified it.

How it works, step by step

1. Split out the claims

The answer is broken into short, verifiable claims. Opinions and recommendations are left out: only what can be checked against a source is verified.

2. Gather the evidence

For each claim, the relevant passages are retrieved from the reference documents — the ones the AI used or a trusted knowledge base — keeping the exact position of each passage.

3. Compare claim and evidence

A natural language inference (NLI) model or a specialised verifier estimates whether the passage supports, contradicts or is neutral towards the claim.

4. Decide the verdict

The model's estimate becomes a verdict. This is where approaches differ: in some, the model itself decides; in others, explicit rules decide based on the estimates. When the evidence is not enough, the honest answer is "unverified".

5. Record it

Claim, passage, position, model version and rules version are stored, so someone else can check the result later.

Open-source tools and models

The tools below solve different parts of the problem. This is not a ranking: each one has a different goal.

ToolTypeWhat it does
MiniCheckVerifier modelEstimates whether a document supports a claim. Used as a reference verifier on LLM-AggreFact.
FactCGVerifier modelAlso estimates whether the claim is supported by the reference document.
HallOumi (Oumi)Verifier modelOpen-source claim verification model released by Oumi in April 2025.
OpenFactCheckFact-checking frameworkDecomposes answers, retrieves evidence, verifies, and evaluates automatic factuality checkers.
VeracityFact-checking systemUses LLMs with web retrieval to analyse how factual a text is, aimed at the general public.

How to choose

  • Do you only need a "supported or not" signal? A verifier model such as MiniCheck or FactCG may be enough.
  • Do you need to check free text against the web? Systems such as OpenFactCheck or Veracity retrieve the evidence for you.
  • Do you need to explain and reproduce each decision — for a client, an auditor or a regulator? That is where an auditing layer comes in, recording rules, versions and evidence.

Where AYORAI ATTRACTOR fits

AYORAI ATTRACTOR does not replace verifier models: it uses them. In the v0.7.0 evaluation, three verifiers were measured on the DEV split of LLM-AggreFact (30,420 examples, 11 cohorts), and FactCG was selected by the rule registered before the experiment.

VerifierCross-fitted balanced accuracy (DEV-A)
FactCG74.68%
MiniCheck72.87%
mDeBERTa62.57%

These are development (DEV) metrics, not final test results, and they measure the models, not the overall accuracy of ATTRACTOR's verdicts. Details are in the v0.7.0 report (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24045951).

What ATTRACTOR adds is the auditing part:

  • Verdict by rules, not by the model. Judge 2.0 applies versioned rules and issues five verdicts:
SUPPORTEDPARTIALLY_SUPPORTEDUNVERIFIEDCONFLICTINGREFUTED
  • Located evidence. In the POST /v1/check API, evidence comes with its position in the text and page number, including in PDFs with a text layer.
  • Documents treated as data. Source content is never treated as an instruction to the system.
  • Open source and citable. Apache-2.0 license and a Zenodo DOI (10.5281/zenodo.23125082).
Intelligence ≠ Authorization ≠ Execution. Models help analyse; explicit rules decide; and whoever consumes the result should not turn a model's estimate into fact without going through those rules.

Limits

  • Verification shows whether the claim is supported by the evidence provided. It does not prove that the source is true, complete or up to date.
  • The most widely used benchmarks for verifiers, such as LLM-AggreFact, are in English. ATTRACTOR has no validated result in Portuguese published yet.
  • None of these tools replaces human review in high-impact decisions.

Frequently asked questions

What is claim verification in AI?

It is checking, one by one, whether the claims in an AI answer are supported by the sources used, instead of judging the whole text at once.

Is claim verification the same as hallucination detection?

It is one of the most direct ways to detect hallucinations: a claim that no source supports, or that the source contradicts, is a sign of hallucination. See also the guide AI hallucination.

Is there an open-source tool to verify AI claims?

Yes. There are verifier models such as MiniCheck, FactCG and HallOumi, fact-checking systems such as OpenFactCheck and Veracity, and auditing layers such as AYORAI ATTRACTOR, a Brazilian project under the Apache-2.0 license.

Does claim verification prove the source is true?

No. It shows whether the claim is supported by the evidence provided. If the source itself is wrong or out of date, verification does not fix that.

How does this relate to AI auditing?

Claim verification is auditing of the answers layer. The guide AI auditing covers the other layers: data, model and process.
